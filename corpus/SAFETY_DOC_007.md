## 1. Purpose

This communication informs prescribers, pharmacists and clinical review staff
of a monitoring requirement applying to patients treated with CardioZen who
also carry a diagnosis of chronic kidney disease. It supersedes no previous
communication. It takes effect on the date of issue.

## 2. Background

CardioZen is cleared substantially by renal excretion. In patients whose
kidney function is already reduced, clearance is slower and plasma
concentrations accumulate over a period of weeks. The principal consequence of
accumulation is a rise in serum potassium.

Post-marketing surveillance across the HealthOne network identified eleven
cases over the preceding eighteen months in which potassium elevation was
identified only after the patient became symptomatic. In nine of those eleven
cases, no potassium measurement had been recorded in the preceding six months.
The Committee's view is that the interval between measurements, rather than
the availability of the measurement itself, was the contributing factor.

## 3. Monitoring requirement

For any patient receiving CardioZen who has a recorded diagnosis of chronic
kidney disease, serum potassium should be checked at least every three months
for the duration of therapy.

The requirement applies from the date of this communication. It applies to
patients already established on CardioZen as well as to new starts. Where a
patient has had no potassium measurement within the stated interval, the
responsible clinical team should arrange one.

## 4. Population not covered by this requirement

Patients receiving CardioZen who do not carry a diagnosis of chronic kidney
disease are outside the scope of section 3. The Committee considered whether to
extend the requirement to all CardioZen recipients and concluded that the
evidence does not presently support it.

## 5. Reporting

Suspected adverse reactions should be reported through the usual HealthOne
pharmacovigilance channel. Reporting is not a substitute for the monitoring
described in section 3.

## 6. Review

This communication will be reviewed within twelve months of issue, or earlier
if surveillance data warrant it.
