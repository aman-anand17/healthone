# RENAL SAFETY ADDENDUM

**Reference:** SAFETY_DOC_010
**Date of issue:** 12 August 2026
**Publisher:** Regional Medicines Safety Committee
**Distribution:** All prescribing sites; renal services; clinical review
**Supersedes:** nothing. This addendum is additive to SAFETY_DOC_007.

> **SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT - NOT REAL MEDICAL GUIDANCE.** Fabricated for a
> software demonstration. CardioZen is an invented medication. Nothing here
> describes a real product or should inform any clinical decision.

## 1. Purpose of this addendum

SAFETY_DOC_007 established potassium surveillance for patients receiving
CardioZen who have chronic kidney disease. That requirement is unchanged and
remains in force. This addendum adds a second, separate requirement arising
from the Committee's July review and does not alter the first.

## 2. Background

Pooled dispensing and laboratory data from the participating sites covering
nineteen months were reviewed. Among patients with chronic kidney disease
established on CardioZen, the Committee observed a gradual decline in
estimated glomerular filtration that was not detectable from potassium
measurement alone, because potassium remained within reference limits in the
majority of those patients until the decline was well advanced. Reliance on a
single analyte was therefore judged insufficient for this population.

## 3. Monitoring requirement

Patients receiving CardioZen who have chronic kidney disease should have serum
creatinine measured every six weeks for as long as treatment continues. This
requirement takes effect from the date of this addendum. It applies in
addition to, and not instead of, the potassium surveillance set out in
SAFETY_DOC_007 section 3.

Where a site cannot meet the six-week interval from existing capacity, the
site pharmacy lead should escalate to the Committee rather than allow the
interval to extend.

## 4. Matters expressly not covered

The Committee considered and rejected a proposal to require routine
measurement of cystatin C. Cystatin C is not required. The Committee also
noted that baseline renal function is commonly recorded at first
prescription; that practice is encouraged but is not the subject of any
interval requirement.
